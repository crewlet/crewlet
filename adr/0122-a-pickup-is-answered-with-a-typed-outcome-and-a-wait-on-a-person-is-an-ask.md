# ADR-0122: A pickup is answered with a typed outcome, and a wait on a person is an ask

- **Status:** accepted
- **Authority:** `internal/pickup`
- **Enforced-by:** `internal/agent/turn.TestACommentAloneDoesNotAnswerAPickup`, `internal/agent/turn.TestAPickupsItemRoundTripsThroughTheStoredReply`, `internal/agent/runner.TestAPickupsSubmissionIsRefusedOverWordsAlone`, `internal/agent/runner.TestAPickupsCollectedRoundCarriesItsTypedAnswer`, `internal/engine.TestAPickupIsAWakeOfItsOwnType`, `internal/engine.TestARunnerIsBuiltPerSeat`, `internal/worklet.TestATurnsTypedAnswerIsAChangeOnTheItemOrAnAsk`, `internal/worklet.TestAnItemGoesQuietOnTheSeatsLastWordUnlessItIsTyped`, `internal/pickup.TestAQuietItemIsRecheckedOnceADay`, `internal/pickup.TestAnUntypedAnswerComesBackOnceADay`, `internal/pickup.TestARecheckSaysWhyItCameBack`, `internal/agent/builtin.TestAWaitOnAPersonIsAnAsk`, `internal/agent/builtin.TestAnAgentsRequestThatNamesAPersonIsAnAsk`, `internal/worklet.TestOnlyWordsThatAskAPersonWithoutAnAskAreARequest`, `internal/worklet.TestTheAdvisorCountsTheRequestsTheToolRefuses`, `internal/worklet.TestEveryEngineDetectorFiresOnItsOwnTimelineAlone`
- **Measured:** on a company running this engine, every one of its six agent seats sat idle, unpaused and within budget for hours while it held 13 ready items, because each item's pickup had been handed once and answered with a comment saying it still waited; for 3 of them the condition the comment named (another item closing, a fix shipping) had been met one to two days earlier, and nothing told the holder. Of 9 waits by agents on the company's one person, 7 were a comment, an @-mention or a `wait_until` date rather than an ask, so they never reached that person's Inbox as a question.
- **Cost-when-tried:** the brief alone. ADR-0112's first brief offered "say why with a comment" among the answers, and 10 of the 13 spent keys were spent under it in two days; its second brief named only typed answers, and the next two pickups were still answered in prose. A brief is advice, and the delivery check accepted any tool call, a comment included, so the advice was never held to.
- **Tag-status:** unreleased

## The decision

**A `work_pickup` turn is delivered only by a typed answer on the item it was handed:** a change to one of the item's fields (its status, its assignee, its content, its relations, a `wait_until` with its reason), or a comment that puts a question to somebody with `ask`. A comment that asks nothing is not a delivery. The turn's obligation is a tool obligation that names the item (`turn.Reply.Item`, stored with the parked run as `tool@<item>`, which an older build refuses to resume rather than read as a plain tool obligation), and the engine reads the answer off its own record: the tracker's history rows that carry the turn's id (`worklet.Reader.TypedAnswer`), never the model's account and never a tool's name. The submission check, the check before the reviewer and the override of the reviewer's `done` all refuse a delivery or a block over words alone with one correction that names the five answers, which the pickup's brief lists too. A read that fails counts as typed and is logged, so the turn falls back to the delivery every turn owes rather than loop on a store it cannot reach.

**An item left without a typed answer comes back once a day.** The ready read stamps each item with when it went quiet for its holder: the latest of the change that last woke the seat, the start it waited until and the seat's own last write on it since, unless that write is a typed answer, which holds the item until a change would wake the seat again. A whole `pickup.RecheckAfter` (24 hours, the stand-up rhythm, a constant) after that instant, the pickup's key gains the instant the recheck came due, so the ledger takes it as new work and the item is handed once; the all-offered memory that spares the ledger expires at the first recheck due. Words, or nothing at all, hold an item for a day and no longer, and an item answered in words before this rule existed comes back by the same rule without anybody touching it. The recheck's brief says the item came back and since when. One expression decides what is typed for both the check and the recheck, so the two cannot disagree.

**A wait on a person is an ask.** An agent's `update_work_item` that sets `wait_until` with a `wait_reason` naming a person seat (by handle, with or without its `@`, or by its name, as a whole word) is refused, naming `ask`, unless the agent holds an open ask to that person on the item. And an agent's comment that says it asks (`asks: true`) and names a person outside its `ask` is refused (ADR-0100); `worklet.RequestedWithoutAsk` is that one predicate, which the comment tools refuse by and the advisor's `person.requests_without_ask` counts by, so the finding names exactly what the tool refuses and an informing mention (`asks: false`) passes both.

## Why the obvious alternative is wrong

**The obvious alternative is a better brief**, and it was tried twice: text cannot hold a turn to an answer the delivery check does not require, and a model that can end on a comment will. **Judging the answer from the calls' names** is the second: `comment_on_work_item` with `ask` is a typed answer and without it is not, and a long run's record keeps its calls' arguments only as far as its bytes allow. The tracker's own rows, keyed by the turn, are exact whatever the run's record kept. **A recheck counted on a clock per item** (every day from when it became ready) would hand an item twice within minutes when a busy seat took its first pickup late in a window; counting from when the item last went quiet ties every recheck to the seat's own last word.

**Reading a person's name in a `wait_reason` is reading prose**, which ADR-0113 refuses for routing. It is accepted here because it routes nothing: a match refuses one call with a remedy the agent can take at once (ask the person), a miss leaves the wait as it was, and the names matched are the ones the chart declares rather than a guess about intent.

## What this does not decide

It does not decide when a pickup is handed, which items are ready or how the cooldown spaces them (ADR-0112). It does not hold an assignment's turn, or any other wake, to a typed answer: an assignment's answer may land in a thread the work came from. It does not make a person's comment or wait an ask; only an agent is held to it. It does not read an agent's words for a request it states it is not making (`asks: false`): the statement is the agent's, as ADR-0113 decides. It does not change how a turn's outcome is counted for the advisor's ledger, where a question is words.
