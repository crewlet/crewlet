# ADR-0122: A pickup is answered with a typed outcome, and a wait on a person is an ask

- **Status:** accepted
- **Authority:** `internal/pickup`
- **Enforced-by:** `internal/agent/turn.TestACommentAloneDoesNotAnswerAPickup`, `internal/agent/turn.TestAPickupsItemRoundTripsThroughTheStoredReply`, `internal/agent/runner.TestAPickupsSubmissionIsRefusedOverWordsAlone`, `internal/agent/runner.TestAPickupsCollectedRoundCarriesItsTypedAnswer`, `internal/engine.TestAPickupIsAWakeOfItsOwnType`, `internal/engine.TestARunnerIsBuiltPerSeat`, `internal/worklet.TestASeatsTypedAnswerIsAChangeOnTheItemOrAnAsk`, `internal/worklet.TestAPickupIsMootOnceItsItemLeavesTheSeat`, `internal/agent/turn.TestAPickupWhoseItemLeftTheSeatEnds`, `internal/engine.TestALostPickupsRunIsHeldToItsItem`, `internal/events/types.TestAPickupAnsweredTypedMovedItsItem`, `internal/worklet.TestAnItemGoesQuietOnTheSeatsLastWord`, `internal/pickup.TestAQuietItemIsRecheckedOnceADay`, `internal/pickup.TestARecheckWaitsADayFromTheItemsLastPickup`, `internal/pickup.TestAnUntypedAnswerComesBackOnceADay`, `internal/pickup.TestARecheckSaysWhyItCameBack`, `internal/agent/builtin.TestAWaitOnAPersonIsAnAsk`, `internal/worklet.TestAPersonIsNamedByHandleOrNameNeverByACommonWord`, `internal/worklet.TestAnAgentsWaitOnAPersonItHasNotAskedIsReady`, `internal/pickup.TestAWaitOnAPersonIsHandedUnderAKeyOfItsOwn`, `internal/agent/builtin.TestAnAgentsRequestThatNamesAPersonIsAnAsk`, `internal/worklet.TestOnlyWordsThatAskAPersonWithoutAnAskAreARequest`, `internal/worklet.TestTheAdvisorCountsTheRequestsTheToolRefuses`, `internal/worklet.TestEveryEngineDetectorFiresOnItsOwnTimelineAlone`
- **Measured:** on a company running this engine, every one of its six agent seats sat idle, unpaused and within budget for hours while it held 13 ready items, because each item's pickup had been handed once and answered with a comment saying it still waited; for 3 of them the condition the comment named (another item closing, a fix shipping) had been met one to two days earlier, and nothing told the holder. Of 9 waits by agents on the company's one person, 7 were a comment, an @-mention or a `wait_until` date rather than an ask, so they never reached that person's Inbox as a question.
- **Cost-when-tried:** the brief alone. ADR-0112's first brief offered "say why with a comment" among the answers, and 10 of the 13 spent keys were spent under it in two days; its second brief named only typed answers, and the next two pickups were still answered in prose. A brief is advice, and the delivery check accepted any tool call, a comment included, so the advice was never held to.
- **Tag-status:** unreleased

## The decision

**A `work_pickup` turn is delivered only by a typed answer on the item it was handed:** a change to one of the item's fields (its status, its assignee, its content, its relations, a `wait_until` with its reason), or a comment that puts a question to somebody with `ask`. A comment that asks nothing is not a delivery. The turn's obligation is a tool obligation that names the item (`turn.Reply.Item`, stored with the parked run as `tool@<item>`, which an older build refuses to resume rather than read as a plain tool obligation), and the engine reads the answer off its own record: the tracker's history rows the seat wrote on the item since the turn began (`worklet.Reader.PickupAnswer`), whichever turn or none wrote them, never the model's account and never a tool's name. A merge queue closes an item as the seat that enqueued the change, outside any turn, and that close answers the pickup. **An item that left the seat while the turn ran owes it nothing:** put in the trash, finished, or held by another seat or by nobody, by somebody else's change, the pickup is moot and every check accepts the turn's ending, and the turn's ending record says how the item was left (`turn_completed`'s `pickup_answer`: `typed`, `removed`, `finished`, `reassigned`, `unanswered` or `unread`). The submission check, the check before the reviewer and the override of the reviewer's `done` all refuse a delivery or a block over words alone, on an item still the seat's, with one correction that names the five answers, which the pickup's brief lists too. A read that fails is accepted as `unread` and logged, so the turn falls back to the delivery every turn owes rather than loop on a store it cannot reach. A run that is lost or stopped, which ends with no loop to check it, is held to the same reading: its delivery stands only where the item was answered typed or left the seat, and is unknown where the read fails.

**An item still ready for its holder comes back once a day.** The ready read stamps each item with when it went quiet for its holder: the latest of the change that last woke the seat, the start it waited until and the seat's own last write on it since, whatever that write was. An answer that takes the item out of the seat's ready set (a question, a blocker, a wait, another assignee) holds it there by itself, and one that moved the pickup's key has the item handed again anyway; one that leaves it ready with the same key, as an edit of its content does, is the seat's last word as words are, since nothing else would ever hand the item back. A whole `pickup.RecheckAfter` (24 hours, the stand-up rhythm, a constant) after that instant, the pickup's key gains the instant the recheck came due, so the ledger takes it as new work and the item is handed once; the all-offered memory that spares the ledger expires at the first recheck due. A recheck is never handed within `pickup.RecheckAfter` of the end of the item's last pickup at the same version, read off the completion ledger's instants, so an item quiet for days before its first pickup is not handed again within the hour. Words, or nothing at all, hold an item for a day and no longer, and an item answered in words before this rule existed comes back by the same rule without anybody touching it. The recheck's brief says the item came back and since when. One expression decides what is typed for the delivery checks and for the advisor's ledger, so the two cannot disagree.

**A wait on a person is an ask.** An agent's `update_work_item` that sets `wait_until` with a `wait_reason` naming a person seat is refused, naming `ask`, unless the agent holds an open ask to that person on the item. A reason names a person by `@handle`, in any case, or by the name the chart gives them, as a whole phrase in the case the chart writes it (`worklet.PersonsNamed`), and never by a bare handle or a name in another case: handles and one-word names are often common words, and "the release will be cut Friday" waits on nobody. A person the directory withholds is not matched, since they cannot be asked. **An agent's wait on a person that nobody asked comes back before its date**, however and whenever it was written: the ready read, by the same matcher, makes ready an item an agent set aside until a later date whose reason names a person its writer holds no open ask to on the item, and the pickup hands it to its holder naming the person, to ask them or restate the reason (`waits_on_person`), under a key of its own so the item is still handed when its date comes. And an agent's comment that says it asks (`asks: true`) and names a person outside its `ask` is refused (ADR-0100); `worklet.RequestedWithoutAsk` is that one predicate, which the comment tools refuse by and the advisor's `person.requests_without_ask` counts by, so the finding names exactly what the tool refuses and an informing mention (`asks: false`) passes both.

## Why the obvious alternative is wrong

**The obvious alternative is a better brief**, and it was tried twice: text cannot hold a turn to an answer the delivery check does not require, and a model that can end on a comment will. **Judging the answer from the calls' names** is the second: `comment_on_work_item` with `ask` is a typed answer and without it is not, and a long run's record keeps its calls' arguments only as far as its bytes allow. The tracker's own rows, keyed by the turn, are exact whatever the run's record kept. **A recheck counted on a clock per item** (every day from when it became ready) would hand an item twice within minutes when a busy seat took its first pickup late in a window; counting from when the item last went quiet ties every recheck to the seat's own last word, and counting no sooner than a day after the item's last pickup ended keeps an item quiet for days before its first pickup from coming back within the hour. **Keying the answer on the turn's id** misses the one write of a turn that lands outside it, a merge queue's close of the item. **Holding an item a typed answer left ready** held it for good: nothing hands such an item back, which is the stall this record exists to end. **Refusing every ending of a turn whose item left the seat** left it no ending at all: a trashed item takes no write, and a typed answer on an item somebody else closed or reassigned undoes their decision.

**Reading a person's name in a `wait_reason` is reading prose**, which ADR-0113 refuses for routing. It is accepted here because it routes nothing: a match refuses one call with a remedy the agent can take at once (ask the person), a miss leaves the wait as it was, and the names matched are the ones the chart declares, written unmistakably, rather than a guess about intent.

## What this does not decide

It does not decide when a pickup is handed, which items are ready or how the cooldown spaces them (ADR-0112). It does not hold an assignment's turn, or any other wake, to a typed answer: an assignment's answer may land in a thread the work came from. It does not make a person's comment or wait an ask; only an agent is held to it. It does not read an agent's words for a request it states it is not making (`asks: false`): the statement is the agent's, as ADR-0113 decides. It changes how a turn's outcome is counted for the advisor's ledger only for a pickup's turn: one the delivery checks read as answered typed counts as moved, an ask included, so `work.pickup_still_waiting` counts as words only the pickups nothing typed answered; on every other turn a question is words.
