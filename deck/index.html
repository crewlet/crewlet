Mission

Make a company something you can write down, run, and change in an afternoon.

Software has been sold by the seat for thirty years. We are making the seat the unit of production instead of the unit of billing. A founder describes a company — its mission, its policies, its chart, who reports to whom — and it is running before lunch, on their own machine, owned outright.

Not a chat window you supervise. Not a workflow you draw. A company: named colleagues who hold seats, remember what they learned, work in the tools your team already opens every morning, and escalate to a person when the decision is above their authority.

The diagnosis

“The core barrier to scaling is not infrastructure, regulation, or talent. It is learning. Most GenAI systems do not retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time.”

MIT Project NANDA — The GenAI Divide, July 2025

We think that is an infrastructure problem wearing a research problem's clothes.

Memory is not a prompt technique. Neither is identity, ownership, a budget that actually stops, or proof that a piece of work reached a person. Those are runtime services — the things an operating system provides so that no application has to.

Nobody ships them. So every serious team rebuilds all five, badly, in private, and calls the result a product.

A model that writes a good answer into its own transcript and never posts it looks, from every metric a dashboard has, exactly like a model that did the job.

the obvious fix, and why it broke

Ask the planner to name the tools it will call, then reconcile the prediction against reality. It failed in both directions, and both are recorded:

too strict  a real delivery read as undelivered, the override fired, and the seat posted the same message twice.

too loose  a turn whose named tool was a wrong guess called nothing but a builtin — “reply hi” produced prose, called no chat tool, and completed silently.

You cannot tune your way out of the wrong question. We deleted the planner: the frame that decides is now the frame that acts, and the engine stopped asking the model what it did.

The fix

A kernel that takes the caller's word for it is not a kernel.

fact one — before any model runs

Who is waiting.

Derived by the engine at dispatch from the trigger's own type. An assignment or a direct mention owes an answer; a schedule fire owes nothing. The model is never asked to declare its own intent, because a turn that declared “skip” on a direct mention reads, to the person who sent it, exactly like the message never arriving.

fact two — recorded as it happens

What actually ran.

The tool loop's own ledger, kept outside the model's conversation — which is also what stops a second round re-firing the first round's side effects. A tool counts as a delivery when it is server-backed and not positively annotated read-only, so the engine ships zero hardcoded knowledge of Slack or Jira and the rule holds for any tool stack.

Three gates, in increasing cost

Gate 1 · at decode time

A claim the record refutes is bounced straight back to the model, listing what is citable — because the failure this catches is usually a model naming the tool it meant to call.

cost: one tool call

Gate 2 · before the reviewer

A no-action nobody asked for ends the turn as skipped; a claim the record refutes loops back carrying an engine-written correction.

cost: no model call at all

Gate 3 · after the reviewer

A “done” on an awaited turn where nothing acted outside the engine is overturned to self_iterate. This is the recorded failure: a reviewer reading a good answer that went nowhere.

cost: one more round

The thesis

The org chart is the execution graph.

Not as a metaphor. A seat's runtime id is a uuid5(namespace, "org name:handle") — derived, not assigned — so every node in a fleet computes the same id with no database and nothing running. That is what lets the node that wins an inbound webhook route the work to a seat it does not itself run, and it is why a seat's diary, its episodes and its profile of a colleague survive every restart and every rebalance.

The chart carries identity, the delegation direction, the lead's roster, and where a blocked turn goes next. Drawing it is the whole of configuring the system. And a human holds a seat the same way — the founder sits at the root, above the top agent, so the chain of responsibility ends at a person by construction rather than by policy.

The surface

Your agents show up in the tools your company already runs.

Six third-party surfaces route end to end — a delivery from any of them wakes the seats it concerns, and the reply goes back where the work lives. Datadog monitor alerts come in the same way.

SlackMattermost JiraConfluence GitHubGitLab Datadog

Ten comments landing on one ticket while a seat is busy become one turn, not ten — drained from the broker, partitioned by conversation, merged into a single digest whose delivery obligation is the strongest of its parts, because a merge must not be able to launder an ask.

You do not adopt a second workplace, and there is no Crewlet inbox for a human to check.

One command per vendor, and we say exactly what it does

mints credentials  Mattermost and GitLab create an account per seat and issue its token.

reports and registers  Jira and GitHub issue no credential on a provisioner's behalf, so those passes report which account each seat authenticates as and register the webhooks. A GitHub App per seat needs a person signed in — twice.

no public URL needed  Mattermost holds one outbound websocket per seat, so that loop runs behind NAT with no tunnel.

A reconcile loop then checks each integration still works, in a finding vocabulary every vendor reports in — and deliberately never tears anything down or rotates a working credential on its own.

What accrues

A seat that has worked here six months is not the seat you hired.

This is our direct answer to the MIT finding. Learning is not a feature bolted onto a turn — it is a deterministic harness that runs after every one, on its own consumer group, so it cannot be starved by traffic and cannot starve it.

It onboards like a new hire

A fresh seat runs a dedicated first-turn pass on its own round budget: it finds its knowledge tools, reads its team's Onboarding pages, captures the conventions with a durable write, and marks itself. A reorg recomputes the chain hash and re-triggers it.

It remembers by use, not by age

A private diary, capped at 500 entries because recall cosines every one at turn start. Past the cap the sweep drops the least-retrieved first — and a “use” only counts when the relevance filter actually selected the entry, never when it merely considered it.

It recalls comparable work

One row per completed turn, vector-indexed, keyed on a work key derived from the trigger's own event ids — so a node that finishes a turn and then lapses does not lose the memory of it.

It learns who it works with

A profile per (observer, subject) pair, built from observed interactions. The seat that briefs your CFO knows how your CFO likes to be briefed.

It writes its own skills

Repeated procedures become drafted, refinable skills the seat loads on demand. Clustering is off by default — a young company has nothing to cluster — and a draft only becomes readable team-wide when a human lead publishes it.

It carries all of it between machines

Memory rides a compacted changelog, so when placement moves a seat to another node its rows are hydrated before its mailbox attaches. Nothing is lost and nothing arrives late.

The artifact

One binary. Then the same binary, more than once.

solo — the design's ordinary case

Nothing to bring up.

The event stream is a NATS JetStream server the process embeds, with no listener and no port. The database is one local file it creates and owns exclusively. CGO_ENABLED=0. The dashboard is compiled into the executable. go install, or a signed binary, and a company runs — no broker, no Postgres, no Node toolchain, no control plane you don't run.

fleet — a config value, not a second product

The same code path.

node.roles picks from ingress, seats and workers; the default is all three. Seat ownership is a TTL lease with a monotonic fencing epoch; placement converges in both directions, so adding a node actually moves seats instead of waiting for something to die. A wedged-but-alive node ends its own process, because a stalled event loop cannot be signalled and its MCP children still hold live per-seat credentials.

The economics of a turn

Frontier model to work. Cheap model to check.

Cost control is architectural here, not a setting. Each phase of a turn chooses its own model, so the expensive one is spent on the work and a cheap one judges it. The reviewer's prompt has no tool catalogue, no policies and no prefetch — and every prompt in the engine sits under a token budget asserted in CI: executor under 2,200, review under 750, the whole turn under 3,000.

The static system + tools prefix is byte-stable within a phase and across a seat's turns, so provider prefix caching absorbs the repetition instead of the guidance being trimmed to fit. Trimming would re-open the incidents the rules exist to close.

And spend is one durable number for the whole company, held in the coordination store rather than per node — so a 10M-token ceiling is 10M however many nodes run it, and it survives a restart.

What bounds the bill

Conversation coalescing — an idle-agent burst worst-cases at two turns, never N.

A round-cap extension judge — a cheap model reads the tool log and grants more rounds only when the seat is progressing, so a static cap stops throwing away nearly-finished work.

A credential pool — least-in-flight rotation, cooldowns on a monotonic clock so an NTP correction neither revives a benched key nor strands a live one, and a fallback chain onto the next model when a pool is spent.

Budgets that actually stop — charged before the call, org-wide then per-seat, with the exhaustion on the event stream.

and one option, stated carefully  Eight coding CLIs ship as first-class model backends, so seats can run on a subscription rather than a metered key, falling back to a key when the window is spent. Subscription plans are generally written for interactive use — we document that plainly and leave the decision to the operator rather than building a margin story on it.

KPMG found 26% of large enterprises have real-time visibility into what their AI costs to operate. We ship the meter: spend rolls up by phase, model, worker, agent and turn, in one implementation the dashboard and the API both read.

Roadmap

Three horizons. Every item is an extension of a seam that already exists.

Now · 0–6 months

Make it startable.

An hour to start a company, an afternoon to change it, a minute to prove it did something.
  • The company registrycrewlet init acme/support-desk fetches a signed, forkable, diffable company — the config is already a versioned document with revisions, diff and rollback.
  • crewlet simulateReplay a trigger corpus against a config with every outbound call stubbed, and get a scorecard per seat. Three of the four pieces ship already: the in-memory broker twin, the provider fakes, a scratch store.
  • Signed work receiptsEvery turn already writes which trigger, which config revision, which models, which tools, what it cost and whether it delivered. Sign it and it is verifiable by anyone holding the company's key.
  • The second knowledge backend, and a local reviewerNotion, Drive, SharePoint or a Markdown repo behind the searcher seam that was built to take them; Bedrock and Vertex behind a provider contract so small a new backend inherits the credential pool and the fallback chain for free.
Next · 6–18 months

Make it compound.

The chart stops being something a founder writes once and becomes an object the company measures and improves — with a human holding the only lever.
  • The reorg loopA weekly pass reads outcome data and emits a config diff with a rationale: this seat is two jobs; this manager is a bottleneck; this reviewer overturns 40% of its executor's work on a model two tiers too cheap. A human activates it. The engine never activates its own reorg.
  • Agents that write their own toolsA seat that keeps hitting the same gap writes the MCP server for it in its sandbox and publishes a draft on its own lease. The draft-and-promote mechanism this needs already ships — for prompts.
  • A seat and skill marketplaceA seat is already an addressable entity with a round-trip API contract, so publishing one — persona, tool grants, phase models, learned skills — is a listing, not a new format.
  • Objectives, not cronA seat declares a measure and a threshold; the scheduler wakes it when the measure moves, stalls, or a horizon approaches, and its one-on-ones hold it to the same record the delivery gate uses.
  • Kubernetes cells, and reaching the humansTwo more sandbox placements in a catalogue built to take them; email, SMS, paging and voice on a notification spine deliberately built before any vendor sat on it.
The frontier · 18–36 months

Make it a network.

One company was the unit. The unit becomes the node.
  • Cross-company A2AA seat in your company asks a seat in someone else's: one ask, one answer, signed at the boundary, scoped by an explicit grant, logged on both sides. Our channel is already an authorization record rather than a transport, which is exactly why a colleague on another node is the ordinary case.
  • An economic layerA seat has a balance, not just a cap, and pays another company's seat for an answered ask. The counter is already durable, fleet-wide and derived from the active config.
  • Attestable companiesAn evidence pack for any window: every decision, on which revision, with which model and credential, what it cost, what the reviewer overturned, which human held the escalation. The log is already append-only with trace ids on the envelope.
  • Sovereign mode, and backtesting a reorgA company with zero egress — local models, local knowledge, local sandbox, a signed reproducible artifact; and last quarter's real triggers replayed against a proposed chart, so the reorg loop stops proposing and starts arguing with evidence.
If this works

The four things we would rather be wrong about than not try.

01 · org design becomes empirical

A company you can fork, diff, benchmark and merge.

The best-performing five-seat product org for a B2B SaaS becomes a versioned artifact with a score, replayed against a shared trigger corpus. Management has never had a benchmark. We already store a company as a diffable revision and already derive a verdict per turn from our own record — a public leaderboard is those two facts plus a corpus.

02 · a market for machine labour

An open protocol for firms that are not made of people.

Companies discover each other, ask for bounded work, verify signed receipts and settle. Not a marketplace we operate — a protocol we give away, with the reference implementation. If it lands, the interesting number stops being ARR and becomes the count of companies that can talk to each other.

03 · the self-optimizing corporation

Structure that improves on a loop, with one human lever.

Each week the engine reads its own outcomes, proposes a reorg, backtests it against replayed history, argues for it with evidence — and waits. A person approves with one compare-and-set, and never fewer than one.

04 · sovereign agent companies

A whole firm, auditable to the token, inside a network no vendor enters.

A signed binary, a local model, no egress, a generated evidence pack. Every hosted competitor is structurally excluded from this market. Our default deployment is most of the way there already, because the broker binds no socket and the store is a file.

Business model

The engine is MIT forever. That is a commercial decision, not a concession.

Every enterprise buyer's first question is whether their source, their credentials and their prompts leave the network, and the only unimpeachable answer is a binary they can read. We do not sell the runtime. We sell the things a single self-hosted binary structurally cannot be — all of them properties of coordination between binaries.

03 · the margin tier

Enterprise

Signed reproducible builds, air-gapped and offline-mirror kits, the generated compliance evidence pack, the Kubernetes and microVM sandbox cells, an SLA, and design partnership on the org model. Sold to exactly the buyers a hosted-only competitor cannot serve at all.

04 · explicitly the option, not the plan

The clearing layer

If the inter-company protocol wins, a basis-point fee on settlement is the largest business on this page by an order of magnitude — and it is worth nothing without the protocol. We underwrite it on the first three tiers. Anyone modelling the round on this line is modelling the wrong company.

Not yet, and we will say so first the honest column

  • No release has shipped this code. The only tag sits on an initial commit with no Go in it. A real v0.1 is the next thing we cut — ask us for a build today and you get one from the branch.
  • No paying customer, no design partner yet. We are pre-revenue and this deck claims no traction we cannot show you in a terminal.
  • The engine composes your ticketing, chat and code host — it does not replace them. There is deliberately no task engine: the tracker is the source of truth and we mirror none of it. What ships is a compose file that stands Mattermost and GitLab up beside the engine, so the whole stack can be yours.
  • Nothing about the pilot-to-production gap is proven at scale by us. The numbers on this deck's earlier slides are the industry's, not ours.
  • No SOC 2, no SSO, no RBAC beyond scoped bearer tokens. The event store is each node's own database, not a fleet-wide immutable ledger.
  • A local sandbox run in direct mode is not a security boundary, and we document it in those words. Isolation means the container or the remote cell.
  • The linux binary needs glibc. CGO_ENABLED=0 does not make it static — the store driver loads its engine with dlopen. No Windows target.
